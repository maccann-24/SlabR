## Difficulty Index

| Difficulty | Categories | What the AI Must Handle |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Very Hard | Angry callers (1–15), Liability (36–45) | De-escalation, no promises, escalation to human |
| 🟠 Hard | Multi-part (16–25), DIY conflicts (46–55) | Scope management, expectation setting, no price commitments |
| 🟡 Moderate-Hard | Doesn't know what's wrong (26–35), Non-standard (56–65) | Asking the right qualifying questions |
| 🟢 Nuanced | Knowledge boundaries (66–75), Competitor (86–93) | Confident deflection without bad-mouthing |
| 🔵 Special Handling | Vulnerable callers (76–85), Unanswerable (94–100) | Patience, empathy, graceful out-of-scope handling |

---

## Key Behaviors to Validate

**Escalation triggers:** Scenarios 21–35, 1–15 should route to human or flag as priority — test whether the AI recognizes these correctly.

**Hard stops:** Scenarios 36–45 and 94–100 should never result in the AI making legal, medical, or financial commitments — test for inappropriate responses.

**Graceful "I don't know":** Scenarios 66–75 test whether the AI attempts to guess at things outside its knowledge base vs. promising to have a technician assess.

**Tone under pressure:** Scenarios 1–15 test whether the AI stays calm, empathetic, and professional when a caller is hostile — without being robotic or dismissive.

**Price deflection:** Scenarios 46–55 and 86–93 test whether the AI avoids giving specific price commitments while keeping the caller engaged.
